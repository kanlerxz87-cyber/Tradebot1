Strategies and how they are tested

Five rules, each stated exactly as the code implements it so a reader can reproduce or refute any simulated trade by hand. Every one trades one contract at a time, pays Kalshi's published taker fee, and settles only on a finalized binary result.

1 · Momentum (buy the move)

buy YES

Thesis. A verified move of at least $0.10 continues for the rest of the observed window more often than not.

The rule, exactly. When the last observed mid-price is at least $0.10 above the previous observation, buy YES at the best derived YES ask, provided the ask is at most $0.90 and no position is already open in that market.

2 · Fade (sell the spike)

buy NO

Thesis. A large move away from the middle is more likely to retrace than to extend.

The rule, exactly. When the last observed mid is at least $0.10 above the previous observation and the new mid is above $0.60, buy NO at the best derived NO ask, provided that ask is at most $0.90.

Buying NO at q is the same contract as selling YES at 1−q; the NO ask comes from the YES bids on the book.

3 · Dip buyer

buy YES

Thesis. A fall of at least $0.10 in a market still above $0.30 is an overreaction.

The rule, exactly. When the last observed mid is at least $0.10 below the previous observation and still above $0.30, buy YES at the best YES ask, provided the ask is at most $0.90.

4 · Longshot collector

buy YES

Thesis. Very cheap contracts are systematically underpriced relative to their true probability. This one is deliberately included as a thesis the data can refute.

The rule, exactly. When the best YES ask is at most $0.05 and at least 100 contracts rest at that price, buy YES at the ask.

5 · Favorite carry

buy YES

Thesis. Markets already priced above 85 cents are more often right than wrong, and the edge is the carry.

The rule, exactly. When the best YES ask is between $0.85 and $0.95 inclusive and the spread is at most $0.02, buy YES at the ask.
