3 · Analysis by trade type

A trade is categorised four ways so the same ledger can answer different questions: which strategy produced it, which topic it came from, what price band it entered at, and how it exited. Those four cuts are what turn raw P&L into a decision about what to keep.

Reading the categories

  • Strategy isolates the rule set. Momentum and fade both fire on alerts but take opposite sides, so together they test whether the market continues or mean-reverts after a $0.10 swing.
  • Topic shows whether an edge is concentrated in one subject (e.g. golf props behaving differently from crypto ladders).
  • Price band is the classic prediction-market effect: cheap longshots and expensive favourites have very different risk profiles.
  • Exit reason separates profit-targets from stop-outs and settlements — a strategy that only ever exits on stops is not working, even if it is flat.

Metrics used, and what they mean

  • Win rate — share of closed trades with positive P&L. Meaningless alone at asymmetric payoffs.
  • Profit factor — gross wins ÷ gross losses. Above 1.0 the book is net profitable.
  • Expectancy — average P&L per trade, in dollars.
  • Max drawdown — the worst peak-to-trough fall of the equity curve, in dollars and percent.
  • Fees paid — modelled fee cost, from Kalshi's published schedule; only the per-series multiplier is approximated (see F1).

5 · How the book is kept honest

Rules

  • Long-only, cash-secured: maximum loss is premium + fees.
  • Fixed-fraction sizing: 2% of current equity per position, capped by visible top-of-book size.
  • Whole contracts only; fractional support exists at Kalshi but rounding down is conservative.
  • Entries pay the ask, exits receive the bid, so the spread is always paid.
  • Settlement pays $1 per winning contract and $0 per loser.
  • At most 12 open positions, one per market per strategy.

Limits of this evidence

  • Paper fills assume the visible size was available; queue position is not modelled.
  • Fees are a flagged model, not the published schedule (F1).
  • The sample is small and recent until the cron job has run for weeks; treat early numbers as instrumentation, not proof.
  • Signals are evaluated once per cycle, so intra-cycle paths are missed (F7).
