Methodology

How a market gets into a topic, how an alert is decided, how the high-ROI score works, how fees are computed, and what the simulator does and does not do. Each rule names its source.

Topic assignment

Kalshi publishes its taxonomy at GET /search/tags_by_categories: 14 categories, with Golf as a tag under Sports and Oil & Gas as a tag under Commodities. There is no Gas and no Culture category, so those two topics are derived:

CultureKalshi category Entertainment or Mentions; or an entertainment tag such as Music, Oscars, Moviescategory / tag

Tags live on the series, not the event — verified by comparing live GET /events (no tags key) with GET /series/{ticker} (has tags). The engine caches series tags in SQLite. Multivariate KXMVE… combos are excluded: one ticker can bundle NFL props and European soccer, so it cannot belong to one topic.

The alert rule

  • Threshold: |Δprice| ≥ $0.10, either direction, checked against the previous sample and against the sample one rolling window (default 60s) earlier — so a fast jump and a slow drift both fire.
  • Price: mid of best YES bid and implied YES ask on a two-sided, uncrossed book; otherwise the last trade; otherwise the best bid. Asks are implied as 1 − opposite best bid because the book returns bids only.
  • Confidence: confirmed when a trade or two two-sided quotes agree; quote-only when the move exists only in a one-sided quote.
  • De-duplication: a per-market cooldown suppresses same-direction repeats; a direction flip always alerts.

High-ROI score

A weighted mean of five components, each in [0,1], computed from values Kalshi actually returns:

ComponentWeightInputs
Magnitude0.30size of the move relative to 4× the threshold
Liquidity0.25contracts buyable within 10c, from the order book
Cost0.20spread and taker fee as a share of contract price
Horizon0.15hours until the market closes
Confirmation0.10trade-backed (1.0) vs quote-only (0.35)

Unknown inputs score conservatively and add a caveat rather than being assumed. The published minimum score for the selective feed is 0.55. This is a ranking of trade quality, not a probability of profit.

Fees

From Kalshi's published fee schedule (effective 2026-07-07):

  • Taker: roundup(M × 0.07 × C × P × (1−P)), default M = 1.
  • Maker: roundup(M × 0.0175 × C × P × (1−P)), default M = 0.
  • No settlement fee, no membership fee.

This project's implementation reproduces every value in the published per-contract and per-100-contract tables (42/42, asserted in the test suite). Rounded fee + position cost up to the cent, then the position cost subtracted, which is how the published table behaves.

Simulation

  • Six strategies, each a distinct trade category: trend following, contrarian, asymmetric payoff, high-probability carry, event driven, and an unfiltered control benchmark.
  • Buys execute at the side's implied ask, sells at that side's best bid — executable prices, not mids.
  • Each strategy starts at $10,000 with its own ledger, so returns are comparable.
  • Positions are opened once and closed once per market; exits are take-profit, stop-loss, max hold, or settlement.
  • Resolution pays $1 per contract for yes/no winners, $0 for losers, and proportional v / 1 − v for scalar settlements, with zero settlement fee.
  • An empty book means no trade, with the rejection reason recorded.
Limitations. 31 Non-Standard Fee rows from Kalshi's July 7, 2026 PDF and explicit GET /series fee_multiplier values are applied, while interleaved PDF rows from KXMVE onward default to M = 1 and the exchange's micro-cent rounding rebate accumulator is not modelled. Simulated results are simulations of a demo environment whose prices Kalshi itself says may not reflect real markets.
